# Impact Printers

CompTIA A+ Core 1 — 220-1201  
Objective 3.8 — Printers

## What You Need to Know

By the end of this lesson, you should understand:

- What an impact / dot-matrix printer is
- How pins, ribbons, and paper work together
- Why impact printers are useful for multipart forms
- What tractor feed paper is
- Common advantages and disadvantages of dot-matrix printers

## What Is It?

Impact printers create output by physically striking the page.

The most common type is a **dot-matrix printer**. It uses a matrix of tiny pins that press through an inked ribbon and leave dots on the paper. Those dots form letters and characters.

## Why Does It Matter?

Impact printers are less common at home now, but they still appear in niche business cases:

- Multipart forms
- Carbon-style copies
- Older warehouse or logistics workflows
- Environments that need low cost per page

Help desk technicians should still recognize how they work and why they are noisy and low-resolution.

## Real-World Analogy

Think of a dot-matrix printer like a tiny set of typewriter hammers.

Each pin taps through an inked ribbon onto the page. Move that pin group across the line, then advance the paper, and you build the full printout one row of dots at a time.

## How It Works

### Dot-Matrix Printing

A dot-matrix print head contains small pins at the front.

Process:

1. The print head moves back and forth across the page
2. Selected pins press into an inked ribbon
3. Ink transfers onto the paper behind the ribbon
4. The paper advances for the next line

If you look closely, printed characters are made of tiny dots. Those dots come from the pins in the print head.

Print heads can get warm, so they often include a heat sink on the back.

### Why Impact Printing Is Useful

Because the pins physically push on the paper, impact printers work well with:

- Carbon copies
- Multipart forms
- Multiple copies created in one pass

They also tend to have a relatively low cost per page for high-volume output.

### Limitations

Dot-matrix printers are:

- Noisy
- Poor for high-resolution graphics or polished documents

That is why they are rarely used in homes and most offices today. They remain useful mainly for specialized jobs.

### Printer Ribbon

The ribbon is usually a cartridge that stretches across the print path.

Key points:

- The ribbon is one continuous loop
- It cycles through the cartridge repeatedly
- Replacement is usually quick: pop out the old cartridge and insert a new one
- Ribbon size is make-and-model specific

### Tractor Feed

Many impact printers use a **tractor feed** to move paper.

Tractor paper has holes on the left and right edges. Those holes line up with tractor pins/sprockets that pull the page straight through the printer.

If the holes are misaligned, paper jams are likely.

Tractor paper may be:

- Continuous
- Perforated into standard page sizes
- Perforated so the hole strips can be removed after printing

Some environments still use green-bar tractor paper, historically common for printed source code and reports.

### Multipart Paper

A major use case for impact printers is **multipart paper**.

One printer pass can create multiple copies.

Older methods used carbon paper. A common modern multipart design uses:

- Micro-encapsulated ink on the back of the first sheet
- Clay on the next sheet that reacts to create the copy

This creates extra copies without directly printing on every sheet.

Note: the chemicals in some multipart papers can irritate skin for some people after repeated handling.

Many organizations now find it easier and cheaper to print extra copies on another printer instead of maintaining multipart impact workflows.

## Common Mix-Ups

### Impact vs Thermal

- Impact = physical strike through a ribbon
- Thermal = heat on special paper, no ribbon

### Tractor Feed vs Regular Paper Tray

Tractor feed uses edged hole paper pulled by sprockets. Regular trays usually feed cut-sheet paper with rollers.

### Ribbon vs Toner / Ink Cartridge

A ribbon is for impact transfer. It is not laser toner or inkjet liquid ink.
