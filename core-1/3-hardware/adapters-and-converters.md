# Adapters and Converters

CompTIA A+ Core 1 — 220-1201  
Objective 3.2 — Cables and Connectors

## What You Need to Know

By the end of this lesson, you should understand:

- The difference between a simple adapter and a signal converter
- Why DVI-D and HDMI can often work with a simple adapter/cable
- Why DVI-A and VGA can often work with a simple adapter
- When analog-to-digital video needs real conversion
- Why USB-to-Ethernet and USB-C-to-USB-A adapters are common
- What a USB hub can provide

## What Is It?

Adapters and converters help when you do not have the exact cable or connector you need.

This lesson covers common examples:

- DVI to HDMI
- DVI to VGA
- USB to Ethernet
- USB-C to USB-A
- USB hubs with many interface types

Some connections are electrically compatible and only need a different connector shape. Others need processing or power to change formats.

## Why Does It Matter?

Help desk technicians live in adapter world:

- Laptop has USB-C only, user has USB-A gear
- Thin laptop has no RJ45, but wired Ethernet is required
- Older projector is VGA, newer laptop is HDMI/DVI
- Temporary classroom or conference setup needs a quick match

Most adapters are meant for temporary use, but some become semi-permanent.

## Real-World Analogy

Think of adapters like travel plug tips and power converters:

- A **simple adapter** changes the plug shape when the electricity already matches
- A **converter** changes the actual power/signal type when the formats do not match

Using the wrong one is like forcing a plug into a wall — it may fit the shape but fail electrically.

## How It Works

### Adapter vs Converter

| Type | Idea |
| --- | --- |
| Adapter / pin-compatible cable | Same kind of signal, different connector ends |
| Converter | Changes formats; may need extra processing or power |

### DVI-D to HDMI

**DVI-D** (digital DVI) and **HDMI** (also digital) are electrically compatible.

That means you can often use:

- A DVI-to-HDMI cable, or
- A small DVI-to-HDMI adapter

and send the video signal without true signal conversion.

### DVI-A to VGA

**DVI-A** (analog DVI) and **VGA** are both analog and can work together with a simple adapter that changes connector shape (15-pin VGA ↔ larger DVI).

Official support note from the lesson: technically only **640 × 480** is officially supported, but a simple adapter is often enough for analog compatibility needs.

### When Conversion Is Required

If you move from **analog VGA** to a **digital DVI** connection, you need signal conversion between those formats.

These converters are useful in a pinch for quick video standard changes.

### USB to Ethernet

Newer thin laptops often have no full-size **RJ45** Ethernet port.

A **USB-to-Ethernet** adapter/converter provides wired networking when:

- Wireless is unavailable or unreliable
- You need a direct wired link for troubleshooting
- You must connect to a device over Ethernet

A very common bag tool for field techs.

### USB-C to USB-A

Many modern laptops dropped full-size **USB-A** ports.

A **USB-C to USB-A** adapter or cable lets you connect older USB-A peripherals.

Forms you may see:

- Short adapter dongle
- Longer cable that moves the USB-A connection away from the laptop

### USB Hub

A **USB hub** plugs into one USB connection and expands to many outputs.

Example hub capabilities from the lesson:

- USB ports
- SD memory card slots
- HDMI
- Ethernet
- Other interface types

Useful when you are unsure which connector you will need, or you want one contingency device for many situations.

## Key Terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Adapter | Changes connector form when signals are compatible |
| Converter | Changes signal format; may need power/processing |
| DVI-D | Digital DVI |
| DVI-A | Analog DVI |
| HDMI | Digital audio/video interface compatible with DVI-D signaling idea |
| VGA | Analog video interface |
| USB-to-Ethernet | Adapter that adds wired networking over USB |
| USB-C to USB-A | Adapter/cable for older USB peripherals on modern ports |
| USB hub | One USB upstream connection expanded to many ports/interfaces |

## Common Mix-Ups

### Adapter vs Converter

Compatible digital-to-digital or analog-to-analog often needs only an adapter. Analog ↔ digital usually needs conversion.

### DVI-D vs DVI-A

- DVI-D pairs naturally with HDMI ideas
- DVI-A pairs naturally with VGA ideas

### “Any video adapter will work”

Match the signal type, not just the plastic shape.

### Hub vs Single-Purpose Adapter

A hub is for flexibility and multiple ports. A single adapter solves one mismatch.
