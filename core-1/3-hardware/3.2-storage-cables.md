# Storage Cables

CompTIA A+ Core 1 — 220-1201  
Objective 3.2 — Cables and Connectors

## What You Need to Know

By the end of this lesson, you should understand:

- What SATA is and how its revisions increased speed
- The difference between SATA data and power connectors
- Why SATA is a one-to-one connection (no daisy chaining)
- What eSATA is used for
- Why SATA and eSATA cables/connectors are not interchangeable

## What Is It?

Modern internal drives often connect with **SATA** cables.

This lesson covers:

- SATA (Serial AT Attachment)
- eSATA (external SATA)

SATA links storage devices to the motherboard with separate data and power connections.

## Why Does It Matter?

Help desk and field technicians install and troubleshoot drives constantly:

- Drive has no power or no data cable
- Motherboard SATA port count limits how many drives you can add
- Someone tries to force an eSATA cable into an internal SATA port
- External drive needs the correct eSATA cable and rear-panel port

## Real-World Analogy

Think of SATA like a private phone line between one desk and one office.

- One data cable = one drive talking to one motherboard port
- No party line / daisy chain
- Power is a separate cord to the same desk
- eSATA is the outdoor version of that phone line — same idea, different plug shape

## How It Works

### SATA — Serial AT Attachment

**SATA** is the common interface for connecting hard drives and similar storage inside a desktop.

SATA revisions increased available speed over time:

| SATA revision | Speed idea from the lesson |
| --- | --- |
| 1.0 | 1.5 Gbps |
| 2.0 | 3 Gbps |
| 3.0 | 6 Gbps |
| 3.2 | 16 Gbps |

Motherboards may label or support different SATA generations (for example SATA 2 and SATA 3 ports on the same board).

### Data and Power Connectors

Storage drives commonly have two SATA physical connections:

| Connector | Pins | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Longer connector | 15 pins | Power |
| Smaller connector | 7 pins | Data |

Some drives also support an older **Molex** power connector in addition to SATA power.

### One-to-One Relationship

SATA is a direct connection:

- One data cable from drive → one motherboard SATA port
- One power cable from drive → power supply

SATA does **not** support daisy chaining multiple drives on one data cable.

So if a motherboard has eight SATA interfaces, it can support eight drives (assuming power and case space allow it).

Motherboard examples from the lesson:

- One board with six SATA connectors
- Another with four SATA data connectors

Matching colored data cables can help keep drive-to-port mapping clear during installs.

### Connecting a SATA Drive

Basic install steps:

1. Connect the SATA **data** cable from the drive to a motherboard SATA port
2. Connect the SATA **power** cable from the power supply to the drive

Both are required for the drive to operate.

### eSATA — External SATA

**eSATA** is the external version of SATA.

Key points:

- Functionality is very similar to internal SATA
- External cable length is about **2 meters**
- Signal idea is effectively the same
- Connector shape is different from internal SATA

Internal SATA data connectors often have a distinctive **L-shaped** keying. eSATA uses a different style, so you cannot plug eSATA into internal SATA or vice versa.

Use:

- An **eSATA cable** for eSATA ports
- An internal **SATA cable** for internal SATA drives

If you see unfamiliar ports on the back of a computer, look closely — they may be eSATA.

## Key Terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| SATA | Serial AT Attachment; common internal drive interface |
| eSATA | External SATA interface |
| SATA data connector | 7-pin connector for drive data |
| SATA power connector | 15-pin connector for drive power |
| Molex | Older drive power connector style |
| One-to-one | One drive data cable per motherboard SATA port |
| Daisy chain | Multiple devices on one cable — not supported by SATA |

## Common Mix-Ups

### SATA Data vs SATA Power

Both are required. Data alone or power alone will not make the drive work.

### SATA vs eSATA

Same family of signaling idea, different connectors and locations (inside vs outside). Cables are not interchangeable.

### Port Count vs Drive Count

Each motherboard SATA data port supports one drive. More drives need more ports (or another controller/adapter).

### Revision Labels

SATA 2 vs SATA 3 on a motherboard refers to supported interface generations/speeds. Match expectations to what the board and drive support.
