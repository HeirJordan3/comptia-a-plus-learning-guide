# Video Cables

CompTIA A+ Core 1 — 220-1201  
Objective 3.2 — Cables and Connectors

## What You Need to Know

By the end of this lesson, you should understand:

- What HDMI carries and its connector basics
- What DisplayPort is and how its locking connector works
- Why DVI has multiple variants (A, D, I, single-link, dual-link)
- What VGA is and why it is analog-only
- How USB-C can carry video and other signals

## What Is It?

Video cables connect computers and other devices to monitors and displays.

This lesson covers common video connection types:

- HDMI
- DisplayPort
- DVI
- VGA
- Video over USB-C

## Why Does It Matter?

Help desk technicians constantly deal with display connection issues:

- Wrong cable for the port
- Audio missing because the cable only carries video
- DisplayPort cable that will not pull out until unlocked
- Older systems with VGA or DVI only
- USB-C docks and adapters that must support the right signal type

Knowing each connector helps you pick the right cable and explain what it can and cannot do.

## Real-World Analogy

Think of video cables like different highway ramps to the same city (the display):

- **HDMI / DisplayPort** = modern ramps that carry cars and music (video + audio)
- **DVI** = mostly cars only (video), with several ramp styles
- **VGA** = an older analog road that gets rough after a short distance
- **USB-C** = one ramp that can carry several kinds of traffic if the road supports it

## How It Works

### HDMI — High-Definition Multimedia Interface

**HDMI** is one of the most common connectors on modern monitors and TVs.

Key points:

- Sends **digital audio and video** on the same connection
- About a **20-meter** maximum range before signal loss can cause blockiness
- Common connector is a **19-pin Type A** connector
- Shape is mostly rectangular with indented bottom corners, so only HDMI fits

### DisplayPort

**DisplayPort** also carries **audio and video** on one connection.

It was one of the first display connections to send data in a **packetized** form, similar in idea to Ethernet networking.

Other notes from the lesson:

- Compatible with HDMI and DVI through a **passive adapter** in many cases
- Desktop cards often use full-size DisplayPort; some devices use **Mini DisplayPort**
- Connector shape is rectangular with one slanted corner, so it will not fit an HDMI port by accident

#### Locking Connector

DisplayPort plugs often lock into place.

- Small locks hold the connector
- A button on the top or side unlocks it
- If a cable will not pull out, it is often DisplayPort — press the unlock button first

### DVI — Digital Visual Interface

**DVI** is common on older systems and still appears on some video cards.

Challenge: one family of connectors, multiple signal types.

| DVI type | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| DVI-A | Analog signals; can be backwards-compatible with VGA ideas |
| DVI-D | Digital only |
| DVI-I | Integrated; can carry digital and analog |
| Single-link | Lower bandwidth path |
| Dual-link | Roughly doubles the traffic capacity of single-link |

Bandwidth idea from the lesson:

- Single-link ≈ **3.7 Gbps**
- Supports traditional HD around **1920 × 1200**

Important difference from HDMI / DisplayPort:

- **DVI carries video only** — no audio through the DVI connection

Always match the computer port and cable type. The right DVI choice depends on what both ends support.

### VGA — Video Graphics Array

**VGA** is a legacy analog video standard.

Connector details:

- 15 pins
- D-shaped connector
- Technically a **DE-15**, often called **DB-15**
- Commonly colored **blue** from the old PC System Design Guide color standards

Other VGA notes:

- **Video only** — no audio
- Analog signal quality tends to degrade past about **5–10 meters** of cable

VGA, DVI, and HDMI look different enough that you can usually tell them apart quickly on a motherboard or adapter.

### Video Over USB-C

**USB-C** is a connector type that can carry many kinds of signals, depending on the device and cable.

Possible traffic over USB-C from the lesson:

- USB power
- USB serial data
- Thunderbolt
- DisplayPort
- HDMI
- **MHL** (Mobile High-Definition Link) for some mobile devices

The same USB-C port may support video only if the device, cable, and destination all support that mode.

## Key Terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| HDMI | Digital audio + video interface; common Type A 19-pin connector |
| DisplayPort | Packetized digital display interface; often locking connector |
| Mini DisplayPort | Smaller DisplayPort connector style |
| DVI | Digital Visual Interface; multiple A/D/I and link variants |
| DVI-D / DVI-A / DVI-I | Digital / analog / integrated DVI types |
| VGA | Legacy analog video standard |
| DB-15 / DE-15 | 15-pin VGA connector naming |
| USB-C | Versatile connector that may carry video and other signals |
| MHL | Mobile High-Definition Link |

## Common Mix-Ups

### HDMI vs DisplayPort

Both can carry audio + video. Shapes differ, and DisplayPort often locks in place.

### DVI vs HDMI

HDMI can include audio. DVI is video-only and has multiple connector/signal variants.

### VGA vs Digital Connections

VGA is analog. HDMI and DisplayPort are digital. Longer VGA runs lose quality faster.

### USB-C as “always video”

USB-C is only a connector. Video works only when the port, cable, and device support it.

| Connection | Remember |
| --- | --- |
| HDMI | Audio + video, ~20 m, Type A |
| DisplayPort | Audio + video, packetized, often locks |
| DVI | Video only; A/D/I and single/dual-link |
| VGA | Analog video, DB-15/DE-15, blue |
| USB-C | May carry video plus many other signals |
