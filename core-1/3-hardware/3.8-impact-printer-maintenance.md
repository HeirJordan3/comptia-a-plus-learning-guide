# Impact Printer Maintenance

CompTIA A+ Core 1 — 220-1201  
Objective 3.8 — Printers

## What You Need to Know

By the end of this lesson, you should understand:

- When and how to replace a dot-matrix ribbon
- When and how to replace a print head
- Why print heads get hot
- How to replace and align tractor-feed paper
- How to avoid paper path jams on continuous forms

## What Is It?

Dot-matrix maintenance is mostly mechanical.

The main tasks are:

- Ribbon replacement
- Print head replacement
- Paper replacement and alignment

There is no toner or liquid ink cartridge like inkjet or laser printers. The ribbon provides the inked surface the pins strike through.

## Why Does It Matter?

Impact printers still show up in niche business workflows. Technicians need to keep them working when:

- Printouts get too light to read
- Characters are missing because pins fail
- Continuous forms jam or print off-alignment

## Real-World Analogy

Think of a dot-matrix printer like a mechanical stamp line.

- The ribbon is the ink pad
- The print head is the stamp mechanism
- The tractor paper is the continuous belt of forms moving underneath

When the ink pad dries out, the stamp wears down, or the belt skews, output quality falls fast.

## How It Works

### Ribbon Replacement

The ribbon is one long loop wrapped inside a cartridge.

As it cycles through the printer repeatedly, ink wears down. Pages get lighter and harder to read. That is the signal to replace the whole ribbon cartridge.

Replacement is usually modular and fast:

1. Pop out the old ribbon cartridge
2. Push the new one into place
3. Resume printing

This often takes less than a minute.

Use the correct ribbon for the printer make and model.

### Print Head Replacement

The print head is a mechanical part with tiny pins.

Over time, one or more pins can stop working, which hurts print quality.

Important safety note:

- Print heads get warm during use
- The back is often a large heat sink
- Let the printer cool before replacement if it was recently used

Some printers require a few screws to remove the print head. Others use a release lever or bar so you can remove it without tools.

Best practice: if you are already replacing the print head, also replace the ribbon. That gives the best output from both new parts.

### Paper Replacement and Alignment

Dot-matrix printers usually do not use single cut sheets.

They use continuous tractor-feed paper with holes on the left and right edges.

When loading paper:

1. Align the tractor holes correctly on both sides
2. Keep the page straight
3. If the paper includes a printed form, align it carefully so output lands in the correct fields
4. Make sure nothing blocks the paper going into or out of the printer

Because the paper is one long continuous feed, any obstruction can shift the path and cause a jam.

Keep the full input and output path clear.

## Common Mix-Ups

### Ribbon vs Print Head

- Ribbon = ink supply getting lighter over time
- Print head = mechanical pins that can fail

### Cut-Sheet vs Tractor Paper

Impact printers often need continuous tractor paper, not normal single sheets from an inkjet tray.

### Fast Swap vs Careful Alignment

Ribbon swaps are usually quick. Paper loading needs careful hole and form alignment.
