# Fiber Connectors

CompTIA A+ Core 1 — 220-1201  
Objective 3.2 — Cables and Connectors

## What You Need to Know

By the end of this lesson, you should understand:

- What an ST fiber connector is and how it locks
- What an SC fiber connector is and how it locks
- What an LC fiber connector is and why it is popular in dense installs
- How ST, SC, and LC compare in size
- Why connector types must match on both ends

## What Is It?

Fiber optic links need standard connectors to attach cables to devices and patch panels.

This lesson covers three common fiber connector types:

- ST
- SC
- LC

All three let you connect and disconnect fiber. The locking style and size differ.

## Why Does It Matter?

In data centers and campus fiber plants, the wrong connector means the link will not connect.

Technicians need to recognize:

- Bayonet vs push-pull vs clip locking
- Which connector is smallest for high-density panels
- When send/receive fibers are paired as one unit

## Real-World Analogy

Think of fiber connectors like different garden-hose fittings:

- **ST** = twist-lock bayonet fitting
- **SC** = push-in latch fitting
- **LC** = smaller clip-on fitting for tight spaces

Same water (light), different coupler shapes.

## How It Works

### ST — Straight Tip

**ST** stands for **Straight Tip**.

Key traits:

- Bayonet-style connector
- Push in and slightly twist to lock
- Untwist, then pull to remove
- Helps keep the fiber from being pulled out accidentally

You can see the straight tip at the end and the bayonet lock on the side.

### SC — Subscriber Connector

**SC** is often called:

- Subscriber Connector
- Square Connector
- Standard Connector

Key traits:

- Push in to lock
- Pull on the outer part of the connector to unlock and remove
- Popular on devices, patch panels, and other data-center gear
- Can be used as a single fiber or as a paired duplex unit for send and receive together

### LC — Lucent Connector

**LC** is a smaller, more compact fiber connector.

Also called:

- Local Connector
- Little Connector

Key traits:

- Holds with a clip on top
- Common when you need more connections in less space
- Often paired so both fibers connect/disconnect together

### Size Comparison

| Connector | Relative size | Locking idea |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ST | Largest of the three | Push and twist bayonet |
| SC | Middle | Push to lock, pull to unlock |
| LC | Smallest | Top clip |

All three do the same basic job: connect fiber. Match the connector type on the cable to the connector type on the device.

## Key Terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| ST | Straight Tip; bayonet fiber connector |
| SC | Subscriber/Square/Standard Connector; push-pull fiber connector |
| LC | Lucent/Local/Little Connector; compact clipped fiber connector |
| Bayonet | Push-and-twist locking style |
| Duplex pair | Two fibers joined so send and receive connect together |

## Common Mix-Ups

### ST vs SC

- ST = twist bayonet
- SC = push/pull latch, more square look

### SC vs LC

Both can be duplex pairs. LC is smaller and uses a clip.

### Connector vs Fiber Type

ST/SC/LC are connector styles. Multimode vs single-mode is the fiber type. You still must match connector form factor on both ends.
