## What Is It?

Network types are categories based mainly on size and location.

Common types include:

- LAN — Local Area Network
- WAN — Wide Area Network
- PAN — Personal Area Network
- MAN — Metropolitan Area Network
- SAN — Storage Area Network
- WLAN — Wireless LAN

## Why Does It Matter?

Help desk technicians use these terms every day when describing where a problem is happening:

- Local building network vs distant site
- Bluetooth headset vs office Wi-Fi
- Storage network vs user LAN

Recognizing the network type helps you choose the right troubleshooting path.

## Real-World Analogy

Think of networks like travel zones:

- PAN = within arm’s reach
- LAN = around your building or campus floor
- MAN = across the same city
- WAN = across cities, states, or countries
- WLAN = wireless version of local access
- SAN = a specialized high-speed warehouse for storage

## How It Works

### LAN — Local Area Network

A **LAN** is a group of networks close to your location.

Examples:

- One floor of a building
- A group of nearby buildings

If you plug into Ethernet or use a local 802.11 wireless network, you are usually on a LAN.

LANs typically provide the fastest everyday speeds because the distance is short.

### WAN — Wide Area Network

A **WAN** connects locations that are far apart, such as:

- Another city
- Another state
- Another country
- Sites around the world

WANs are often slower than LANs because of long-distance challenges.

WAN technologies may include:

- Point-to-point serial links
- MPLS (Multiprotocol Label Switching)
- Terrestrial networks on Earth
- Non-terrestrial networks using satellites

### PAN — Personal Area Network

A **PAN** is a short-range network close to a person.

Common PAN technologies:

- Bluetooth
- Infrared
- NFC (Near Field Communication)

Examples:

- Hands-free audio in a car
- Wireless headsets
- Fitness devices syncing to a phone
- Phone media integration nearby

### MAN — Metropolitan Area Network

A **MAN** sits between a LAN and a WAN.

It usually covers one geographic area such as a city, connecting multiple remote sites around that city.

MANs were traditionally proprietary, but many now use standardized Ethernet, often called **Metro Ethernet**.

Local governments may also build MANs with their own fiber because they control right-of-way for underground pathways.

### SAN — Storage Area Network

A **SAN** is a specialized high-speed network for storage.

To connected systems, SAN storage can look and feel like local storage.

SANs often support efficient **block-level access**, which is useful when changing small parts of large files.

Because SANs move a lot of data, they are usually:

- Isolated from the regular user network
- Connected with high-speed links

### WLAN — Wireless LAN

A **WLAN** is a wireless local area network, usually based on 802.11.

It lets users move around a home, office, or campus while staying connected on laptops, phones, and other wireless devices.

Larger areas can be covered by adding more access points.

## Common Mix-Ups

### LAN vs WAN

- LAN = close and usually faster
- WAN = far and often slower

### LAN vs WLAN

- LAN = local network category
- WLAN = wireless form of local networking

### PAN vs LAN

- PAN = personal short-range accessories
- LAN = building/campus local network

### SAN vs Regular File Share

A SAN is a specialized high-speed storage network, often with block-level access, not just a normal shared folder.
